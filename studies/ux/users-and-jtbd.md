# Users and Jobs to Be Done

## Evidence versus hypothesis

**Evidence:** OTW’s model already captures follows, sport familiarity, emotional preferences, available minutes, mood, watched events, and spoiler tolerance. The prioritization brief states the concrete trigger: “I missed 30 sporting events this weekend.” Industry research indicates highlights and personalization are common fan needs ([IBM 2025 Sports Survey](https://filecache.mediaroom.com/mr5mr_ibmnewsroom/199459/IBM_Sport_Survey_Report_2025.pdf)).

**Hypothesis:** the highest-pain early users are asynchronous, multi-event fans with replay access—not all sports fans. Their success criterion is a confident viewing decision made quickly, not discovering an abstract excitement score.

## Primary persona: the time-boxed catch-up fan

Behavioral characteristics, not invented demographics:

- Misses events because of schedule or time zone.
- Has access to some full replays and highlights.
- Follows at least one entity deeply and samples others.
- Deliberately avoids results before deciding what to watch.
- Has 10–120 minutes and wants to spend them well.

**Core job:** “When events finish before I can watch, help me choose the best use of my available time while preserving uncertainty.”

**Desired outcomes:** decide in under 60 seconds; avoid accidental outcome inference; reach playable content; feel the recommendation matched their tastes.

## Secondary personas

### The cross-sport sampler

Understands one sport well and is curious about others. Needs comparable recommendations plus enough context to understand why an event matters. “Historic” must not assume expert knowledge.

### The loyal completionist

Always watches a followed team/player/driver, regardless of quality. OTW should prioritize *format and order*, not pretend the event is optional.

### The spoiler-flexible efficiency seeker

Will accept result or pivotal-moment disclosure to save more time. Needs session-level control, never a permanent assumption.

## Jobs and forces

| Situation | Functional job | Emotional/social job | Current workaround | Risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Morning after overnight games | Pick one replay | Preserve suspense | Mute alerts; cautiously open streamer | Streamer thumbnail/score leaks |
| Several weekend events | Build a queue | Avoid regret/FOMO | Ask friends; scan forums | Advice contains results |
| Only 20 minutes free | Choose highlights | Stay connected | YouTube/search | Titles and thumbnails spoil |
| New sport/competition | Judge significance | Feel oriented, not ignorant | News recap | Jargon and context gaps |
| Followed entity competed | Decide full vs condensed | Honor fandom efficiently | Watch everything | Time cost |

## Cross-sport mental model

The common object is an **event worth consuming**, but granularity differs:

- **F1:** race is primary; qualifying/sprint are related sessions. A replay length is relatively predictable. Grid position, fastest lap, podium imagery, retirement flags, and highlight thumbnails can spoil.
- **Tennis:** match is primary but duration is highly variable; tournament round and best-of format matter. Set count, match duration, bracket advancement, and “five-set epic” leak outcomes or shape.
- **Ice hockey:** game is primary; regular season versus playoff context matters. Final/period scores, overtime/shootout labels, goalie pull, series state after the game, and highlight imagery leak.

Use one action taxonomy—**Full replay / Condensed or extended highlights / Short highlights / Skip**—but display verified duration and availability per provider. “Condensed” is not universally available.

## Onboarding hypothesis

Progressive onboarding should ask only:

1. Select F1, tennis, and/or ice hockey.
2. Follow teams, players, drivers, competitions, or series.
3. Choose protection: **No hints**, **Light context**, or **Results allowed**.
4. Ask available time in the catch-up session, not as a profile field.

Do not ask users to rate seven emotional dimensions on first use. Infer cautiously from feedback, then expose editable preferences. The current five-point “follow strength” is valuable internally but cognitively heavy; use labels such as “Never miss” and “Interested.”

## Research questions

- Does “worth your time” solve a repeated weekly problem?
- Is a high recommendation itself an unacceptable spoiler?
- Which unit do fans choose: event, session, match, series, or tournament day?
- Do users trust OTW enough to bypass their habitual score app?
- Is verified replay availability necessary for recommendation value?
- Does the universal action taxonomy survive all three sports?
